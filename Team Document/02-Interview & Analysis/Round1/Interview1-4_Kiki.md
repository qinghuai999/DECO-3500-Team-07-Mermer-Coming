# Paper:

**Insight 1 — High gaming engagement does not necessarily mean problematic gaming**

A player spending a lot of time on games does not necessarily mean that their gaming is problematic. The important difference is whether gaming remains balanced with other parts of life or becomes difficult to control and causes conflicts with daily activities.

**Insight 2 — Social connection is an important value of gaming**

Gaming is not only entertainment. It can help players build and maintain social relationships, receive emotional support, broaden their social networks, and reduce loneliness.

**Insight 3 — Social relationships can both support wellbeing and make problematic gaming harder to leave**

Social relationships formed through gaming are not simply positive or negative. For highly obsessive players, close gaming relationships may provide belonging and emotional support, but these same relationships can also make problematic gaming harder to leave.

**Insight 4 — Difficulty stopping gaming is not only about playing time**

Players may find it difficult to stop not simply because they want “more gaming time”, but because they have not reached a satisfying stopping point or a sense of closure in the game.

**Insight 5 — Social gaming can make disengagement more difficult**

Multiplayer and social gaming can make it harder for players to stop because they may feel social pressure or responsibility toward other players.
